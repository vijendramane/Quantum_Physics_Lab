# 01 - What is Gravity?

## Introduction

Gravity is one of the four fundamental forces of nature and is responsible for keeping planets in orbit, causing objects to fall toward Earth, and shaping the structure of the universe.

Interestingly, although gravity is something we experience every day, physicists still do not fully understand its deepest nature. We have theories that describe gravity with incredible accuracy, but the fundamental question of *what gravity actually is* remains one of the biggest mysteries in science.

---
 
## Newton's Theory of Gravity

In 1687, Sir Isaac Newton proposed the Law of Universal Gravitation.

[
F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
]

Where: 

* (F) = Gravitational force
* (G) = Gravitational constant
* (m_1, m_2) = Masses of two objects
* (r) = Distance between them

### Main Idea

Every object in the universe attracts every other object.

Examples:

* Earth attracts you toward its surface.
* The Moon is attracted by Earth.
* Earth is attracted by the Sun.

The greater the mass, the stronger the gravitational attraction.

The greater the distance, the weaker the attraction.

### Example

When you drop a ball:

* Newton explained that Earth exerts a gravitational force on the ball.
* The ball accelerates toward Earth because of that force.

### Limitation

Newton's theory describes **how gravity behaves**, but it does not explain **why gravity exists** or how gravitational effects are transmitted through space.

---

## Einstein's Theory of Gravity

In 1915, Albert Einstein introduced the General Theory of Relativity.

Einstein proposed a completely different view:

> Gravity is not a force.

Instead:

> Mass and energy curve spacetime, and objects move along those curves.

### What is Spacetime?

Einstein combined space and time into a single concept called **spacetime**.

Massive objects such as planets, stars, and black holes bend this spacetime.

Objects moving nearby naturally follow these curved paths.

### Rubber Sheet Analogy

Imagine placing a heavy bowling ball on a stretched rubber sheet.

The sheet bends around the ball.

If you roll a marble nearby, the marble moves toward the bowling ball because the surface is curved.

Einstein suggested that space and time behave in a similar way.

### Example

According to Einstein:

* The Sun bends spacetime around it.
* Earth moves along this curved spacetime.
* This motion appears to us as an orbit.

No invisible pulling force is required.

| Cannot explain black holes                     | Predicts black holes                                 |
| Cannot explain time dilation                   | Predicts time dilation                               |

## Real-World Evidence for Einstein's Theory

### GPS Satellites

GPS satellites orbit far above Earth where gravity is slightly weaker.

As a result, time passes slightly faster for satellite clocks than for clocks on Earth's surface.

Engineers must apply Einstein's corrections every day.

Without General Relativity, GPS navigation would quickly become inaccurate.

### Black Holes

Einstein's equations predicted the existence of black holes decades before they were observed.

Today black holes have been directly imaged and studied.

### Gravitational Lensing

Massive galaxies can bend light passing nearby.

This effect, known as gravitational lensing, has been observed many times and matches Einstein's predictions.

---

## Which Theory is Correct?

Both theories are correct within their range of applicability.

For most engineering and everyday calculations:

* Newton's theory is sufficient.

For extremely strong gravitational fields, very high precision measurements, black holes, and cosmology:

* Einstein's theory is required.

In fact, Newton's theory can be viewed as a simplified approximation of Einstein's theory.

---

## What is Gravity Really?

The honest answer is:

> We do not yet know.

Einstein's General Relativity is currently our best description of gravity.

However, General Relativity does not fully combine with Quantum Mechanics.

One of the biggest goals in modern physics is to develop a theory of **Quantum Gravity** that unifies both frameworks.

Possible approaches include:

* String Theory
* Loop Quantum Gravity
* Emergent Gravity

So far, no final answer has been confirmed.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Newton described gravity as a force between masses.
* Einstein described gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
* Einstein's theory explains phenomena that Newton's theory cannot.
* GPS systems, black holes, and gravitational lensing support Einstein's theory.
* The fundamental nature of gravity remains one of the greatest unsolved problems in physics.

Gravity is therefore not only one of the most familiar phenomena in our daily lives but also one of the deepest mysteries in modern science.

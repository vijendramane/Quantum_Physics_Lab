# 02 - Quantum Entanglement

## Introduction 
 
Quantum Entanglement is one of the most fascinating and mysterious phenomena in modern physics.   
 
Albert Einstein famously referred to it as:
 
> "Spooky action at a distance."

Entanglement occurs when two or more quantum particles become connected in such a way that their properties are linked, regardless of the distance separating them.
 
If one particle is measured, information about the other particle becomes immediately known. 
 
This behavior appears strange because it seems to connect particles across vast distances.

--- 
 
## What is Quantum Entanglement?

Imagine creating two special quantum particles.

After they become entangled:

* Particle A does not have an independent state.
* Particle B does not have an independent state.
* The pair must be described as a single quantum system.

The particles remain connected even if they are separated by large distances.

---

## Simple Example

Imagine two magical coins.

Classical coins behave independently:

Coin A = Heads

Coin B = Tails

or

Coin A = Heads

Coin B = Heads

There is no special relationship.

Entangled quantum coins behave differently.

Before observation:

* Coin A has no definite value.
* Coin B has no definite value.

After observing Coin A:

* Coin B instantly becomes correlated with the result.

This does not mean information travels faster than light.

Instead, quantum mechanics describes the pair as one combined system.

---

## Bell States

The most famous entangled states are called Bell States.

One example is:

|Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩) / √2

This means:

50% chance of obtaining:

|00⟩

and

50% chance of obtaining:

|11⟩

Both particles always agree.

---

## Why is Entanglement Important?

Quantum entanglement is the foundation of:

* Quantum Computing
* Quantum Cryptography
* Quantum Teleportation
* Quantum Communication
* Quantum Information Theory

Many modern quantum technologies rely on entanglement.

---

## Einstein's Objection

Einstein did not believe quantum mechanics was complete.

He argued that entanglement suggested hidden information must exist.

In 1964, physicist John Bell developed Bell's Theorem.

Experiments performed later showed that quantum mechanics correctly predicts entanglement.

Today entanglement has been confirmed experimentally many times.

## Key Takeaways

* Entanglement links quantum particles into a single system.
* Measuring one particle affects the description of the entire system.
* Entanglement has been experimentally verified.
* It is a core component of quantum computing and quantum communication.
* Quantum entanglement challenged classical ideas about reality and remains one of the most remarkable discoveries in physics.

Quantum entanglement demonstrates that the universe behaves in ways that are often very different from our everyday intuition.
